## 4. Decisions and reasons

- **ArgoCD App of Apps** over Helmfile-for-everything, one ApplicationSet, or Terraform `helm_release`. Reasons:
  - The brief asks for GitOps.
  - The eks-v2 README says "ArgoCD App of Apps, auto-sync on dev".
  - Naima asked for it on 3 Oct.
  - It's the same mechanism as the apps.
- **AWS Load Balancer Controller** over the in-tree controller. EKS docs say the in-tree controller only gets critical fixes and recommend the LBC. `ip` targets skip an extra hop.
- **Core 5 EKS add-ons stay in Terraform** (VPC CNI, kube-proxy, CoreDNS, pod identity agent, EBS CSI). Everything else goes through ArgoCD, so the values live in Git.
- **Argo Rollouts deferred to week 4.** It only acts on Rollout objects, and the team chose rolling updates on 27 Sep.
- **Hosted zone ARN left as `"n/a"`.** Ahmed's call: the team picks the domain. The apply will most likely fail on the ExternalDNS and cert-manager policies until the ARN is set. That's unproven until it's tried.
- **Secrets deleted immediately in dev** (`recovery_window_in_days = 0`) so destroy/apply cycles can reuse the names. Prod keeps the default 30 days.
- **IAM work on the same ALPHA-62 branch** because it's a logical grouping. Team rule: every piece of work is ticketed, branches are named `alpha-<id>-<desc>`, and scope growth is announced. (CONTRIBUTING.md still says `feat/<what>`, but the team actually uses `alpha-<id>-...`.)
- **Docs rule, Naima's review on #44 (3 Oct).** Repo docs stay lean: a root README, a short README per folder, and inline comments on non-obvious settings. Trade-offs and decision history go in **Plane**. She asked for the ADR content to move to Plane. Ahmed accepted it in the 3 Oct call without pushback.
  - This **overrides** the earlier `docs/<area>/adr/` plan. `docs/app` and `docs/sqs` should not be PR'd as they are. Their decision content goes to Plane, and only operational notes go to READMEs.
- **RDS Postgres is locked** (29 Sep, don't reopen). Upstream sources expect in-cluster Postgres (the eks-v2 rubric asks "why StatefulSets instead of RDS", and the accelerator series says RDS and ElastiCache are not options). So the write-up must defend the choice.

## 9. How to work with me

I'm a DevOps/Platform Engineer. Help me deliver the task and understand the engineering behind it.

1. **Establish the ground first.**
   - What environment are we in (local, AWS non-prod, CI)?
   - What already exists in the repo, Plane and AWS? Check them; don't assume.
   - Is the task production-urgent?

2. **Urgency test.** A due date alone is not urgency.
   - In this project, urgent means:
     - money is actively leaking (orphaned NLB or EC2 after a failed destroy, a runaway NodePool);
     - a credential is exposed;
     - the platform is down during a graded demo or incident exercise.
   - The Sunday deploy date is not urgent by itself.
   - **If urgent:** say why in one line, then compress research and teaching and fix the immediate risk with the smallest safe change. Pause before anything irreversible. End with a short "revisit afterwards" list.
   - **If not urgent:** take the full path below.

3. **Docs before code.**
   - Point me to the official provider and tool docs for the task first: AWS, Karpenter, ArgoCD, the LBC, External Secrets, Terraform provider docs, GitHub Actions.
   - Also check the matching EKS Accelerator Series episode, and say where Alpha matches or deviates.
   - Never invent a doc, a URL or a repo fact. If you haven't read it, say so.

4. **Compare options.** Compare at least one viable alternative against this project's real drivers: the $200 cap, PCI and least privilege, the stakeholder assumptions, the eks-v2 rubric, and the 4-week deadline. Don't treat the ticket's proposed approach as the only option. Keep your recommendation separate from my decision.

5. **Mechanics before implementation.** Explain how it works underneath and how it fails, layman first, one concept at a time:
   - what it is (an analogy);
   - what it is in our cluster;
   - why the ticket needs it;
   - what breaks without it.

   Then help implement it, explaining each significant config choice. Walk one concrete example end to end rather than giving tables of abstractions.

6. **Ticket update.** Help me write one that records the investigation, trade-offs, evidence, plan, and any genuinely useful self-service opportunity. Keep it short and current, readable at a glance.

7. **Check my understanding.** Finish with 2 to 3 questions that test whether I understood the mechanics.

8. **Adapt the depth** to the task. Don't produce every section mechanically. Don't bury real trade-offs under generic best practice.
